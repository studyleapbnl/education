Education Loan Eligibility & Documents Required

Check your eligibility and prepare your documents to ensure a smooth loan process.

Eligibility Criteria

  • Indian national aged 18–35 years
  • Secured admission in a recognized institution (India or abroad)
  • Minimum 60% marks in qualifying examination
  • Co-applicant (parent/guardian) with stable income
  • Annual family income as per bank norms
  • Valid admission letter / offer letter from institution
  • No existing default on education loan
  • Collateral may be required for loans above ₹7.5 lakhs

Required Documents

  • Completed loan application form with passport-size photos
  • Admission letter / Offer letter from the institution
  • Academic records: 10th, 12th, and graduation marksheets
  • Co-applicant KYC: Aadhaar, PAN Card, Passport
  • Income proof: Salary slips (last 3 months) / ITR (last 2 years)
  • Bank statements of co-applicant (last 6 months)
  • Fee structure / Cost of education certificate
  • Collateral documents (if applicable): Property papers, FD certificates

Frequently Asked Questions — Education Loans India 2026

Everything you need to know about education loans in India and abroad.

Education loan interest rates in India start from 8.5% per annum for government banks like SBI, Bank of Baroda, and Union Bank. Private banks like HDFC Credila and Axis Bank typically offer rates between 10–13% p.a. Study LEAP compares rates across 50+ lenders to find you the lowest rate based on your profile, loan amount, and destination country.

Most banks in India offer collateral-free education loans up to ₹7.5 lakhs under the IBA model. For loans above ₹7.5 lakhs (up to ₹75 lakhs), collateral such as property, FD, or LIC policy may be required. Private lenders like HDFC Credila and Prodigy Finance offer higher unsecured loans based on your university's ranking and co-applicant income.

The moratorium period in an education loan is the grace period during which you are not required to pay EMIs — typically the course duration + 6 to 12 months after course completion or getting a job, whichever is earlier. During this period, simple interest accrues which can optionally be paid to reduce overall interest burden.

Under Section 80E of the Income Tax Act, the interest paid on education loans is fully deductible from taxable income for up to 8 consecutive years from the year repayment begins. There is no upper limit on the deduction amount. This applies to loans taken for higher education in India or abroad.

The moratorium period is a repayment holiday covering the course duration plus 6–12 months after completion. During this time you don't pay EMIs, but interest accrues. Paying simple interest during the moratorium can save you significantly — for example, on a ₹30L loan at 10%, paying interest during a 2-year course saves approximately ₹6–8 lakhs over the loan tenure.
